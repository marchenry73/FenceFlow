# Real supplier prices, 1 October 2026

Both suppliers quoted the same two jobs. Read off their PDFs, not estimated.

## THE CORRECTION THAT MATTERS

**A 4 ft fence is not a cheaper 6 ft fence. It costs MORE per panel.**

My estimate assumed a 4 ft panel was a shorter privacy panel at about 75% of the 6 ft price
(I guessed $39.26). Both suppliers quoted something else entirely:

| | 6 ft panel | 4 ft panel | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flori Fence | $54.15 T&G privacy | **$61.74** Melrose flat top, 2 rails | +14% |
| Hartford | $55.00 Pro Series privacy | **$71.50** Clearwater closed-top picket | +30% |

Neither sells a "4 ft privacy panel". At 4 ft the product becomes a **semi-privacy / picket
style** -- which is exactly what the photograph he sent shows: the short section has vertical
slats with gaps, not solid boards. A decorative panel costs more to make than a plain solid
one, so the price goes UP as the fence gets shorter.

**Every 4 ft estimate I gave before this file is wrong and too low.** The post guess was the
only one close: I said 75%, Flori charges 79.6% ($13.18 against $16.56).

## For the cancellation clause

Both suppliers' terms are the contractor's real exposure, and they are what the clause should
be built around rather than a made-up percentage:

- **Flori: 60% up front, all sales final, 15% restocking if a return is accepted at all, and
  custom orders are not returnable.**
- **Hartford: prepay, in-stock only, 3% card fee.**

So once he has placed the order, a customer cancelling leaves him holding material he cannot
return in full, and in the custom case cannot return at all. A cancellation clause that
recovers "costs actually incurred" is both fair and exactly right here -- it needs no invented
percentage, because the supplier's own invoice is the evidence.
