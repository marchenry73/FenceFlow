# Reply to the customer asking about licensing and insurance

You said: be transparent, not insured or licensed, working towards it, Fence
Solutions Legacy, you do fence on the side, learned it since COVID, happy to
send pictures of past work.

Here it is. Copy the email; the notes underneath are for you, not for them.

---

## The email

> **Subject:** Re: your fence quote — straight answer on licensing and insurance
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for asking directly — it's a fair question and you deserve a straight
> answer rather than a dodge.
>
> **I'm not licensed or insured yet.** I'm working toward both, but I'm not
> going to tell you I have something I don't. I'd rather lose the job than have
> you find that out later.
>
> Here's the honest picture so you can decide with your eyes open.
>
> I started doing fence work during COVID and I've been at it ever since. It's
> work I do alongside my regular job, which means I'm not running a crew and
> I'm not trying to be the cheapest or the biggest — I take on a small number of
> jobs and I do them properly. I'd be glad to send you photos of fences I've
> built, and I'm happy for you to talk to people I've done work for.
>
> What being uninsured means for you, plainly: if something were damaged or
> someone were hurt on your property during the work, there's no policy behind
> it. That's a real thing to weigh, and it's the main reason some people choose
> a bigger outfit. I won't talk you out of that.
>
> What I can offer instead:
>
> - **Photos of past work**, so you're judging the fence and not the paperwork.
> - **References** you can call.
> - **No money up front** beyond what's needed for materials, and I'll show you
>   the receipts for those.
> - **I stand behind the work.** If something isn't right after I'm done, I come
>   back and fix it. That's not a document, it's just how I work — but you'll
>   have it in writing from me.
>
> Your quote stands as sent either way, and there's no pressure from me. If
> you'd rather go with someone licensed and insured, I genuinely understand, and
> there are no hard feelings.
>
> If you'd like the photos, say the word and I'll send them over today.
>
> Thanks for considering me,
>
> Marc
> Fence Solutions Legacy

## Why it's written this way

**The answer comes second, not last.** The single worst version of this email
buries "no" under three paragraphs of credentials-adjacent reassurance. Saying
it immediately is what makes the rest believable.

**It names the risk to them, in their terms.** You volunteered transparency, and
transparency that leaves out the part the customer actually cares about isn't
transparency. Saying it yourself is far stronger than having them work it out —
and it's the sentence that makes the rest of the email credible.

**It doesn't apologise twice.** One clear statement, then straight to what you
*do* offer. Repeated apology reads as someone who knows they're underqualified.

**It replaces paperwork with evidence.** Photos, references, receipts, and
coming back to fix things are the things you actually have. They're not a
substitute for insurance and the email doesn't pretend they are — they're a
different kind of assurance, offered honestly.

**It gives them an easy exit.** Counter-intuitive, but "I understand if you go
elsewhere" removes the pressure that makes people stop replying. People who
feel cornered go quiet; people who feel free often come back.

## Two things to check before you send

**Licensing may not be optional, depending on the job.** In Florida, fence
installation is often treated as a specialty trade and several counties —
Hillsborough included — have their own contractor registration rules, and many
fence jobs need a **permit** pulled regardless of who does the work. Worth ten
minutes on the Hillsborough County contractor licensing page before you send
this, because "I'm working toward it" is a much better sentence if you know
exactly what *it* is. I'd rather flag that than let you say it and find out
after.

**General liability is cheaper than most people expect.** For a one-person
operation doing residential fence work it's often a few hundred dollars a year,
not thousands. If it turns out to be affordable, this email becomes a much
easier one to send next time — and you'd be able to answer this question with a
yes.

Neither of these changes the email. They change what you're able to write in it
six months from now.
