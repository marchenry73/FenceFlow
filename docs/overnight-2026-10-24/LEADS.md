# FenceFlow lead database (research date 2026-10-24)

Nothing in this file was sent, submitted or contacted. Every row comes from the company's own public website page listed in its "Source" column, read on 2026-10-24. No logins, no forms, no email.

## Read this first: limits of the data

- **Crew size was not published by a single company.** No site stated a crew count or fleet size. "Size signals" therefore holds only what the site actually said (years in business, number of offices, project counts, review counts). All of those are the company's own claims. Where I scored "small-to-mid", it is an inference, labelled as such.
- **Owner names are rare.** Only the names shown in the Owner column were stated on a page. Names that appeared only inside customer reviews are marked as review-only and should not be used as a greeting until confirmed.
- **Blank or "not stated" means the page did not say.** I did not fill gaps from memory.
- **Page text was pulled through a summarising fetch tool**, not read by eye. Spot-check a phone or email against the live page before you use it. The Source URL is the page to check.
- **Emails** are listed only where the page printed one. Several are free-mail addresses (gmail, aol). Treat them as business contacts only.
- **robots.txt:** I only fetched ordinary public pages and ran no crawler. I did not separately read each site's robots.txt.
- **Hot leads: none found.** See the hot-lead section. The absence is not proof nobody is shopping.
- **Search tool caveat.** The search tool could not reach Reddit (blocked), and software queries returned mostly vendor blogs. Owner-forum evidence was therefore not searchable from here.

## Scoring (fit for FenceFlow, 1 to 5)

- **5:** real, current site; estimate request is phone or a plain contact form (looks manual); no customer portal or online quote tool seen; family or owner-run; size looks small to mid.
- **4:** same, but with one soft negative (more offices, unknown update date, a lean-commercial mix, or very small).
- **3:** a signal that it already has quoting tooling, is multi-office or high volume, or the site looks stale (copyright 2019 to 2024).
- **2:** already shows an online instant-quote tool plus other systems, or looks too large or organised to need us.
- **Unrated:** could not verify, franchise, lead-gen site, or closing.

A site that already has an instant-quote widget (MySalesman, MyBudgetQuote and similar) lowers the score for the "customer-facing quotes" angle, but those companies can still fit tracing-based estimating and crew time tracking. They are noted.

## Cross-cutting observations from the sample

- **None of the pages I read described a customer portal for quote approval.** The only portal I saw was Hage Fence's invoice-payment link. Several companies run instant-quote widgets on third-party tools (MySalesman at Elite and Hage, MyBudgetQuote at J Rivers, Eagle Fence and King's). Those appear to be ballpark-price lead capture. None described a signed or approved quote, or an electronic deposit. That gap is where "customer-approvable quotes" fits, but it is my reading of the pages, not something any company said.
- **Most sites route to a contact form or a phone number**, and several promise a response time (Family Fence "within two days", Sunset Fence "2 to 3 business days", Pinpoint "24 hours", Bay To Bay "same-day quotes"). Response time is a natural hook for the estimating-speed email.
- **No page mentioned hand-written estimates.** "Hand-written" is an assumption about back-office process, not a finding.
- **Several sites look unmaintained** (copyright 2019 to 2024: Arden, Dynamite, Advanced Fence 'N' More, B&B, Fence Mule, H&H). Check these are still trading before outreach.

## Outreach strategy (short)

1. **Lead with the owner's own story, locally.** "I run a fence company in [city] and built this for my own crews" is the strongest line available and cannot be copied by a software vendor. Confirm it is accurate before use.
2. **Do not mass-email.** Start with the top-ten shortlist plus the named-owner leads (Fence Fanatics, Alamo Fence, K&A Fencing, Elite, Sarasota Fence). Phone first where only a phone number is published (AJ Fence, Sarasota Fence). A call to a small office is more likely to reach the owner than an email.
3. **Email only addresses a business itself published**, one per company, one idea per message. Include a clear way to decline, the sender's real name and a business mailing address. This is ordinary cold-email hygiene, not legal advice. Check current rules before sending.
4. **Send from the FenceFlow business address** (marc@fenceflowapp.com), not the personal Gmail.
5. **Be aware of the name collision.** My notes record that a separate company, FenceFlow LLC (fenceflow.com), powers a franchise with a Riverview branch. A local fence owner may assume the two are the same. Say in the first line who you are and that this is a software product.
6. **Pick the angle by what the site shows.**
   - Phone or form only, with a "free estimate" promise: estimating speed (Draft 1).
   - Multiple counties or crews: crew time tracking (Draft 2).
   - Already has an instant-quote widget, or promises a quick response: customer-facing quotes (Draft 3).
7. **Offer a low-friction next step**, such as running one of their recent jobs through the app for comparison. Do not claim features that are not live. Add real trial terms and pricing where the drafts have placeholders, since I do not have them.
8. **Track outcomes** per company (called, replied, demo, declined) so declined contacts are never contacted again.
