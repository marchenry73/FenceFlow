# What changed on August 17, 2026

Nine things went in. Four were bugs that would have cost money or credibility
with a customer; the rest were groundwork for other companies using this.

---

## The two that actually mattered

### 1. The app was refund-blind almost everywhere

Only the job-detail money panel and the PDF subtracted refunds. Everything else
used the gross figure — the total that ever came in, ignoring anything handed
back.

What that looked like in practice:

- **The customer status message** said `Received so far: $1,000.00` after $400
  had been refunded to them. The app was telling your customer you kept money
  you gave back.
- **Job detail** showed *"Total paid so far"* (gross) a few inches above
  *"Paid so far"* (net). Two labels that mean the same thing, two different
  numbers, no way to know which was right.
- **Reports → Collected** — the figure that goes on a tax return — was gross.
- Also the pipeline stage and its "Paid" chip, the outstanding-jobs table, the
  profit breakdown, and the review-template picker, which was offering the
  big-job wording for a job refunded down to nothing.

Now one rule everywhere: anything that says "paid" means `netPaid` — gross
minus refunds, floored at zero. Nine tests cover each place that used to
disagree.

### 2. The push trigger was carrying a master database key

The database trigger that sends notifications authenticated itself with the
**service_role key** — a key that bypasses every security policy on every table
for every company. It was sitting in plain text inside the trigger definition,
just to say "a job changed."

It now sends a purpose-built secret that can do exactly one thing: ask for a
notification. If it leaks, the cost is a spam notification instead of every
company's customer records.

Verified rather than assumed: no header and a wrong header both return 401, the
right one returns 200, and firing it live against the real database returned 200
with the trigger conditions unchanged.

> **The old key still needs rotating.** Removing it from the trigger doesn't
> un-expose it. Supabase → Settings → API → Rotate. Nothing in the app uses it.

## Things a customer or another company would notice

### 3. Export Everything

Settings → Export Your Data. A zip of spreadsheets — jobs, payments, expenses,
crew hours, change orders, materials, crew — that opens in Excel, Numbers or
Sheets.

CSV rather than a database file on purpose: a `.db` backup only opens in this
app, which is worth little if the reason you're exporting is that you're
leaving it.

**It also closed a real security hole.** Every value in an export was typed by
somebody — a customer name, a job note. A spreadsheet executes any cell starting
with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@`. So a note reading `=HYPERLINK("evil.com","Click")`
became a live link in *your* spreadsheet when you opened your own export. Those
are defused now. The old expense CSV had the same hole.

### 4. Spanish that reads like a person wrote it

Every Spanish string in the app was written without accents — `resena` instead
of `reseña` (which isn't a word), plus `informacion`, `Direccion`, `estan`,
`valido`, `dias`. To a Spanish speaker the quote and contract read as machine
output, and that's the first impression of the company sending it.

There was never a technical reason. Fixed across the PDF labels and all three
message templates.

The contract also said **labour**, **colour** and **authorised** — British
spellings on a contract a US homeowner signs.

### 5. The contract is missing a legally required notice

Most states require a home-improvement contract to state, in specific wording
and often a specific type size, that the customer can cancel within three
business days. The federal Cooling-Off Rule adds requirements for contracts
signed at the customer's home — which is most fence jobs.

Leaving it out can make the contract unenforceable and carry a penalty on its
own.

I did **not** write the language — it's state-specific and I'm not a lawyer.
There's now a block in the contract that says plainly it must be replaced before
use, so it can't ship unnoticed.

### 6. The materials catalog is usable at its real size

Hundreds of items behind fence-type tabs with no search. Finding one meant
guessing which tab it was filed under.

- **Search across every fence type at once.** Someone typing "cedar" wants the
  cedar, wherever it lives.
- **Unpriced items are surfaced.** A zero price isn't a decision, it's a blank —
  and it silently drags any estimate using it below cost. The count sits at the
  top and taps through to just those. Those rows read "No price", not
  "$0.00 / ea", because $0.00 looks deliberate.
- **Real labels.** Enums were shown as "CHAIN LINK" and "LINE POST" in thirty-odd
  places. Some of those strings reach a customer's quote.

## Groundwork

### 7. The home screen stopped doing N queries

The "still owed" total loaded line items, fence runs and change orders
separately for **every won job**, every time any payment changed. Now three
queries total.

Also collapsed three hand-copied copies of the linear-feet calculation into one.
They agreed today, but three copies of a rule is three chances for the home
total to stop matching the job it was added up from.

### 8. Backups

`scripts/backup-cloud.mjs` dumps every table to timestamped JSON. Verified
against the live database: 467 rows across 18 tables.

Two deliberate safety properties: it **refuses to write inside this repository**
(which is public — a backup committed there would publish every company's
customer data), and it **exits with an error when it captures nothing**, so a
scheduled run that quietly breaks reads as a failure instead of a folder of
empty files that looks fine until the day you need it.

`BACKUP.md` covers what is *not* protected: no schema, no photos, no user
accounts. Even a clean restore means everyone signs up again.

### 9. Privacy policy and terms now match reality

- **Stripe wasn't mentioned at all**, though it processes card payments.
- The three address-lookup services weren't mentioned, though they receive the
  customer address as you type it.
- It said deleting a job removes it. It hasn't since the trash bin — deleted
  records are recoverable until the owner empties it. Anyone deleting a customer
  record on request needed to know there's a second step.
- The "last updated" date was generated in JavaScript, so the page always
  claimed to have changed today. People use that date to know whether the terms
  moved since they last read them.
