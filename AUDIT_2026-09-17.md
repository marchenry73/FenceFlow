# Production audit, 17 September 2026

Seven parallel reviews (login, security, the job lifecycle, the Android app,
the website and office, competitors, and setup/testing/releases), then fixes.
Everything below that says "fixed" was built, tested and shipped the same day
unless the line says otherwise.

## 1. What condition FenceFlow is in

Good, with two things standing between it and a paying customer: the app is
still a **test build** (unsigned for release), and there is **no separate test
environment** — every test runs against the live database. Both need you (a
keystore password, and a free second Supabase organization).

The product itself held up under an adversarial pass: no way was found for one
company to read another's data, webhook signatures and quote tokens are sound,
and no secret keys are in the app or the website. The holes that were found
were inside a company — a role seeing more than it should — and are now closed.

## 3. Bugs found that nobody had reported

- **Office showed nothing at all.** The jobs request asked for a column that has
  never existed, so every tab read as empty. Caused by this audit; found and
  fixed the same hour.
- **Phone could not reach the cloud, and sign-out did nothing.** The new rule
  that signatures can never be overwritten also blocked the *first* signature
  upload, so the file never landed, the phone counted it unsynced for ever, and
  sign-out refused. Fixed; signatures still cannot be overwritten.
- **A customer could be asked to sign twice.** Approving online and signing in
  the app were tracked separately.
- **Typed feet reset to zero while typing**, which made Suggested quantities
  read nothing.
- **A closed fence loop never showed the closing side's length** (the total was
  right, the side was invisible).
- **Undo looked broken** when there was nothing left to undo: it did nothing and
  said nothing.
- **An English-only sentence** sat on the shift-approval screen.
- **Imported catalog prices counted as confirmed**, so a quote could go out on an
  unchecked number. Fix in progress.

## 4. Security findings, all fixed and proved

Each fix was proved twice: the attack now fails, and the legitimate path still
works, both inside rolled-back transactions.

1. **Company takeover.** Anyone signed in could claim a company that had been
   invited but not yet claimed, by setting a value on their own account. Now it
   takes server-side invitation data.
2. **Pay rates leaked** to crew, foremen and salespeople.
3. Salespeople could record payments and refunds.
4. Crew and accountants could fake a customer's quote approval, and reset the
   phone-number check on a quote link.
5. An owner could undo a hold you placed on their account.
6. Crew could read your private admin notes, the lead link, and pricing settings.
7. Crew could overwrite a signed contract's signature image.
8. The quote phone check could be brute-forced with parallel attempts.
9. Anyone in a company could add an employee row and set its pay rate.
10. A patch had quietly dropped the money-permission guard on job costing; restored.

## 5. What was automated or added

- Nine new alerts: HOA, permit, 811 never requested, hours correction awaiting
  the worker's answer, disputed hours, material over budget, job running long,
  payment failed, scheduled but blocked.
- A ready-or-blocked checklist per job.
- A daily briefing split into Sales, Production, Crews, Materials, Money, Risks,
  every line clickable.
- Sales follow-ups (server side): lead not contacted, quote not opened, quote
  opened not approved, approved with no deposit. Every rule off until switched
  on, with quiet hours, a daily cap, no repeats, and opt-out respected.
- **Re-approval when the drawing changes**: a material change (feet, corners,
  gates) to an approved job withdraws the approval, keeps the old one as
  history, and leaves money untouched. Cosmetic edits do not.
- Pay by the foot, split evenly between the per-foot workers on a job.
- Overtime now agrees between phone and office (over 40 paid hours a week, 1.5x,
  hourly workers only).
- Test-fixture jobs hidden from the office and no longer downloaded to phones.
- New companies start with an empty catalog instead of 91 invented prices.
- Scheduled releases: `publish-release.mjs --at "<date time>"`.

## 6. Performance

The office asked for every column of every job. It now asks for the 78 it
actually reads, with a test that fails if a column a screen reads is missing
from the list, and the two unbounded event logs are windowed to 18 months. The
dashboard paints as soon as its own data is in rather than waiting for
everything. Measured earlier at 1,500 jobs: the database was never the
bottleneck; the payload was.

## 7. Competitors

Full notes in the audit folder. The short version: Jobber and Housecall Pro win
on polish and integrations, ArcSite on drawing, ServiceTitan on enterprise
depth. None of them do fence takeoff, a customer-facing fence visual, and crew
pay by the foot in one product. The gaps worth closing are speed-to-lead and
automated follow-up (now built) and a customer portal.
